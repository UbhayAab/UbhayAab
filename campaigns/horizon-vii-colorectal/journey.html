The frame

The trial data on colorectal cancer is published everywhere. What isn’t published is what six senior oncologists actually do when the patient in front of them doesn’t match the trial population, the biosimilar costs a third as much, and the family is paying out of pocket. That’s the two hours.

Every email ladders up to that sentence, and carries at least one of three proof pillars. Rotate them; never send without one.

Access

MD Anderson GI faculty, live, free, from your living room, on a Saturday. No registration fee, no flight, no three days away from patients.

Realism

Two unscripted real-world cases plus twenty-five minutes of open panel disagreement. Sixty of the hundred and twenty minutes are unscripted.

India context

Biosimilars, cost and access, and the patient side — via AIIMS, NCI Nagpur, Max Saket, Sir HN Reliance and Jarurat Care.

Four decision points, in the order a contact hits them. Each one reads a different signal and needs a different kind of email — not a louder version of the same one.

Build the exit conditions before the entry conditions in every automation. Doing it the other way round is how someone gets “you were thirty seconds away” four hours after they registered — the single most common way a campaign like this embarrasses itself.

Guardrails

This campaign sits in a regulated space, with a named product on the agenda and two pharmaceutical supporters. Five things to hold to.

Keep every email educational, never promotional. The agenda names cetuximab and includes a biosimilars segment, with Merck and Zydus as supporters. Describe the clinical question, never a product benefit. Supporters appear as “Supported by” with logos only — no claims, and no brand attached to a subject line.

Never imply CME accreditation unless it is real. If it is accredited, name the body and the credit hours. If not, say “certificate of participation” and nothing more.

Only send where there is a lawful basis. Prior registration, explicit opt-in, or an existing relationship. Partner lists must be opt-in at source. Never buy a list — it will burn the sending domain for every future edition.

Confirm the case presentations are de-identified before any case detail reaches marketing copy. Keep the marketing description of both cases generic and non-identifying.

No individual medical advice from the campaign inbox, particularly on patient replies. Point to the treating team and to Jarurat Care’s patient support services instead. The reply playbook has the standard wording.

Blocked — needs your input

M8 (T−3) cannot ship yet. That email is entirely social proof from Editions I–VI, and the block is still placeholders. The same block appears in compressed form in M1, M6 and X4, where it degrades gracefully — but M8 does not.

What’s needed per edition: title, date, a one-line summary of no more than fifteen words, keynote, attendees, recording_url. Plus aggregate totals and at least two attributed testimonials.

Two notes for when you fill it: summaries should name an outcome, not a topic — “where six oncologists disagreed on adjuvant duration” earns a click, “explored immunotherapy” does not. And a working recording link from a past edition is stronger proof than any summary.
